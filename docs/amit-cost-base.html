AMIT cost base adjustments:
how to know they’re right

There are two different questions hiding in “is this correct?”. The app can prove the numbers are consistent. Only Vanguard’s annual tax statement can prove the numbers are true.

Every adjustment pairs with a distribution, the per-fund totals sum to the published figures, and the cost-base arithmetic closes. All checks pass on the live database.

Question 2
! Actually right

The amounts are only as good as their source. That means the AMMA — the AMIT member annual statement from Vanguard — not the app.

01

What the number is supposed to be

One signed, fund-level dollar amount per distribution — never per unit, never a closing balance, never written onto a single parcel.

Source
AMMA / ShareSight “Cost base adj.” on each distribution line
→
Stored
One row per distribution stock + date + signed amount, 4dp
→
Applied
Pro-rata across parcels weighted on cost base, once only

Negative reduces cost base, positive adds to it. A 0.00 row is a real answer — the statement prints zero every year — while a blank field means “not recorded”. Dropping the zeros would make “did I record every distribution?” unanswerable.

4 Conservation at disposal

The per-parcel shares must sum back to the fund total, and the whole total is apportioned once — never re-applied per historical row, which would make the answer depend on entry order.

  • Open positions use the same basis: whole-fund cost base less the pre-AMIT cost base disposals already took.
  • Realised and unrealised gains therefore sit on one footing — no “sold on post, holding on pre” split.
  • The toggle is honest: Post-AMIT moves the Investment table and the Trades summary by exactly $15.26, asserted in tests/amit-basis.test.ts.
03

The external check — the only real proof

Do this once a year, per fund, when the AMMA arrives. It takes a few minutes and closes the question completely.

  1. Open the fund’s AMIT member annual statement (Vanguard portal or the registry’s tax statement).
  2. Find “AMIT cost base net amount” — the yearly net adjustment, not the per-unit figure.
  3. Compare it to that fund’s row in check 3. Match → nothing to do. Differ → edit the individual distribution.
  4. If the statement shows a zero, keep the 0.00 row. Do not delete it: the row is the evidence you checked.
